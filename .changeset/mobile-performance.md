Faster on a phone. A finger tap tolerates a 10 px drift before it counts as a pan (a mouse click keeps its 4 px), a finger that goes past 10 px and comes back no longer taps, the finger left after one finger of a pinch is cancelled keeps panning, a tap on empty map clears the selection only if nothing else selected an item in the double-tap window, and a tap that selects an item opens no hover balloon (the selected card already names it). Points outside the box are not drawn and the rest are drawn in one path per colour, so overlapping points now stack by colour (every point of one colour above or below every point of another) instead of in node order. The scene and the item labels are now built in time slices of a few tens of milliseconds instead of one long task: a new `spec` draws nothing (and `onViewChange` does not fire) until its scene is ready, which on a large corpus is a few hundred milliseconds after mount; a tuning or palette change on the same `spec` keeps the current scene on screen until the new one is ready, and item labels appear once placed. Scene building itself is about three times faster (territory outlines and family colours), with the same output. `relatedItems` indexes a spec once and reuses the scene's index, so selecting an item no longer walks every edge; a spec changed in place keeps its old index, so pass a new spec object after changing its edges or nodes. Switching `spec` away and straight back before the other one was built now refits, as it did before slicing. The time slicing and the batched point drawing add weight: the gzip size ceiling rises to 34,887 bytes (the main entry measures 34,787).
